# Hi, I'm Jesse 👋

## And this is what I'm building.

---

### The Simple Version

Imagine you're living in a neighborhood where:

- **Food grows everywhere** — in parks, empty lots, front yards — and anyone can eat from it
- **Energy comes from the sun**, stored in the ground, and powers homes through winter
- **The internet works even if Comcast goes down**, because neighbors' routers talk to each other directly
- **Nobody owns the system** — it belongs to everyone who uses it
- **The people who build it are the people who benefit from it**

That's what I'm working toward. It's called **OpenRoot**.

---

### Why does this matter?

Right now, almost everything we need to survive — food, energy, internet, housing — is controlled by big companies. We pay them every month just to exist. If they raise prices, we pay more. If they shut off service, we lose access. We have no say.

**What if we didn't need them?**

What if a neighborhood could grow its own food, generate its own power, run its own internet, and build its own homes — using materials that other people threw away?

That's the question OpenRoot is trying to answer.

### What's actually being built?

I'm working on several pieces that all fit together:

#### 🌱 Permaculture Systems
Permaculture is a way of designing landscapes that work like natural ecosystems. Instead of planting one crop and hoping it survives, you design a system where plants, animals, water, and soil all support each other. Think of it like a forest — nobody fertilizes a forest, nobody waters it, nobody sprays it. It just works, because everything in it has a role.

I want to design neighborhoods that work the same way.

#### ☀️ Solar Thermal Energy
Most solar panels you've seen convert sunlight into electricity. They're expensive and only work when the sun is shining.

I'm designing a system where **concrete absorbs heat from the sun**, stores it underground like a battery, and releases it slowly through winter. The concrete is made from aerated glass fiber reinforced cement — basically, concrete with tiny air bubbles that make it lightweight and excellent at trapping heat.

The goal: **a home that heats itself**, year-round, with no gas bill, no electric bill, no firewood.

#### 📡 Mesh Internet
When you get internet from Comcast, there's one wire coming into your house, and Comcast controls it. Cut the wire, no internet.

A **mesh network** is different. Instead of one wire, you have a web of small routers — like the ones people have in their houses — that talk to each other. Each router passes signals to the next one. If one breaks, the signal finds another path. Nobody controls it because everybody owns a piece of it.

I'm building these from recycled electronics — routers, phones, computers that businesses were going to throw away. Their trash becomes our infrastructure.

#### 🏠 Indestructible Housing
I work in concrete. I know how to pour it, shape it, and make it last a hundred years. I'm designing **ferrocement dome panels** — curved concrete pieces that bolt together like LEGOs to create dome homes. Domes are the strongest shape in nature. They resist hurricanes, earthquakes, and fire. And because the panels are modular, anyone can assemble them.

#### 🤝 A Way to Organize Without Bosses
OpenRoot isn't a company with a CEO. It's an open-source project, meaning anyone can contribute, anyone can use it, and no one person controls it. The rules are written down as **axioms** — basic principles everyone agrees to — rather than imposed from the top down.

Think of it like Wikipedia: nobody owns it, nobody profits from owning it, but everyone benefits from it existing.

### Where does the money come from?

I'm building this as a **business** under my LLC, but the business model is different from normal:

1. **Electronics recycling** — I collect old electronics from law firms and businesses, securely destroy their data, and either recycle the materials or repurpose the hardware into mesh network nodes
2. **Internet service** — the mesh network provides internet to neighbors at $15/month instead of $60/month from Comcast
3. **Construction** — I pour concrete for a living and can formalize that as a contracting business under the LLC
4. **Grants** — government programs fund rural broadband, renewable energy, and community development in underserved areas. The same "bad neighborhoods" nobody wants to invest in are the ones that qualify for these grants
5. **Open-source donations** — people who believe in the project can support it through the GitHub funding page

The money goes back into the system — buying more land, more tools, more nodes. It compounds.

### What's OpenRoot named after?

**Root** — as in, going back to the roots. The fundamentals. What do humans actually need to thrive? Food, shelter, energy, community, knowledge. Everything else is added complexity.

**Open** — as in, open to everyone. Not owned. Not gated. Not behind a paywall.

OpenRoot = going back to the roots of what humans need, and making it open to all.

### The Big Picture

I'm one guy with a phone, working from a small town, building something I believe could change how communities work. Not because I want to get rich — although the system is designed to be profitable — but because I think the way we're currently living is unsustainable.

We pay companies for things we could produce ourselves. We throw away materials we could build with. We depend on systems we don't control.

**OpenRoot is about taking that control back — together, as a community, starting from the ground up.**
