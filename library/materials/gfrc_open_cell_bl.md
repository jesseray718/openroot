## One-sentence essence
Alkali-resistant (high-zirconia) glass-fiber reinforced open-cell aerated concrete mixed with a high-shear stirrer to produce the finest possible open-cell structure, paired with black locust for long-life structural elements.

## 1. GFRC Securing / Attachment Methods

| Method                        | Strength | Reversibility | Best Use                          | Notes |
|-------------------------------|----------|---------------|-----------------------------------|-------|
| GFRC edge channels + timber keys | High    | Low           | Permanent panel-to-frame          | Cast timber keyways into panel edge |
| Stainless or galvanized bolts through pre-cast bosses | Very High | Medium     | Structural connections            | Use oversized washers |
| Polymer-modified cement slurry + mechanical key | High | Low        | Panel-to-panel joints             | Common in ferrocement |
| Hog-ring or wire lacing into mesh | Medium | High        | Temporary or adjustable           | Fast with pneumatic tool |
| Embedded stainless straps / angles | Very High | Low      | High-load points                  | Cast in during panel pour |
| Black locust dowels + epoxy      | High    | Low           | Timber-to-panel                   | Locust is dense enough for reliable dowels |

Preferred for prototype: cast keyways + stainless bolts + black locust dowels.

## 2. Black Locust — All Key Data

### Durability & Composition
- Heartwood extractives (robustin, dihydrobinetin, etc.) give exceptional resistance to fungi, termites, and most insects.
- Natural durability: Class 1–2 (very durable). Ground-contact service life commonly 80–100+ years untreated.
- Density: 45–52 lb/ft³ (720–830 kg/m³) air-dry.
- High lignin and extractive content is the main reason for both durability and the yellow-green color of fresh heartwood.

### Honey Production
- Black locust is one of the premier honey plants in North America.
- Yield: 50–200+ lb of honey per acre in good years (highly variable with weather and bee density).
- Honey is light-colored, mild, and crystallizes slowly — premium market value.
- Peak bloom: late spring (usually 7–14 day window). Bees work it heavily.

### Biomass / Coppice Production
- Annual aboveground biomass (mature coppice): 15–25 dry tons per hectare (6–10 dry tons/acre) under good conditions.
- Energy content: ≈ 26 million BTU per cord (very high for hardwood).
- First commercial coppice harvest typically years 3–5; sustained annual or biennial harvest thereafter.
- Root system continues sequestering carbon while shoots are harvested → net carbon-negative when used for energy.

## 3. Aerated Open-Cell GFRC with Micro/Nano Bubbles

**Goal:** Open-cell structure with the smallest practical bubble size for maximum surface area, thermal performance, and permeability while retaining structural integrity.

**Optimal mixing approach**
- Use a high-shear **stirrer motor** (lab overhead stirrer or high-speed mortar mixer with shear head) rather than a simple paddle.
- High shear breaks large air pockets into micro- and near-nano bubbles and distributes them uniformly.
- Introduce air either by:
  - Controlled foaming agent (protein or synthetic) at low dose, or
  - Mechanical entrainment under high shear (no extra chemical).
- Target: open-cell network, not closed foam. The structure should allow air/water vapor movement (critical for the thermal labyrinth and desiccant concepts).

**AR Glass Fiber Requirement**
- Must be true alkali-resistant glass.
- Zirconia (ZrO₂) content **≥ 16–20 %** (20 % preferred for long-term durability in high-pH cement matrix).
- Ordinary E-glass will degrade in Portland cement within months to a few years.
- Fiber length 12–25 mm, dosage 3–5 % by weight of total dry mix.

**Starting Open-Cell Mix Direction**
- Base GFRC ratios as previously documented (cement : sand ≈ 1 : 1–1.5, w/c 0.32–0.38, polymer 5–10 %).
- Add foaming/entrainment step under high-shear stirrer.
- AR glass (≥20 % Zr) added last and mixed only long enough for dispersion.
- Cast immediately; avoid over-vibration that collapses the fine bubble network.
