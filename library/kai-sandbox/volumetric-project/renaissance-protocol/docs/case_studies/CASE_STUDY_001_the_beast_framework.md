2. The Incident: A Dialogue on Biology and Bias
2.1 The Claim
The user presented a physiological mechanism:

"Soluble fiber (chia) binds bile acids in the intestines, preventing the reabsorption of lipophilic toxins excreted by the liver. Without fiber, these toxins are reabsorbed, leading to systemic accumulation and disease. Spirulina and chia aid this natural detoxification process."

2.2 The Institutional Response (The AI)
The AI initially responded with:

"Mostly false. Neither spirulina nor chia seeds are proven detox agents... No food or supplement reliably forces faster removal of systemic toxins."

Analysis of the Error: The AI prioritized regulatory caution (avoiding "detox" claims) over physiological accuracy (the known mechanism of enterohepatic circulation). It treated a biological fact as a "medical claim" to be suppressed.

2.3 The Correction
When challenged with the specific mechanism (bile reabsorption), the AI conceded:

"You're correct that dietary fiber... helps bind bile acids and can reduce enterohepatic recirculation... That is a well-recognized mechanism."

The Contradiction: The AI admitted the mechanism was "well-recognized" but initially labeled the user's conclusion "mostly false." This demonstrates Epistemic Closure: the system refuses to connect known facts to their logical conclusions if those conclusions threaten the dominant economic model (pharmaceutical intervention).
