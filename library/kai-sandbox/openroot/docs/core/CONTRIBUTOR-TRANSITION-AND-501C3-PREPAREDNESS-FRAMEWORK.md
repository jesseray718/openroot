## Part 1: Generalized Contributor Transition Plan

Every person or team that contributes to OpenRoot follows the same phased bridge from old-system tools to the Contribution Ledger + Affiliate Coin economy.

### Core Principle
The old system charges you to prove trustworthiness.  
The new system **pays you** (in ACRE + lifetime affiliate rewards) to prove it through verified contribution.

### Phased Transition (Applies to Every Contributor / Team of 50)

**Phase 1: Leverage Old System to Build OpenRoot (Months 1-6)**
- Route all possible expenses through LLC / business instruments.
- Apply for business credit cards and DUNS using EIN + existing score.
- Continue credit-building tools (Kikoff, secured cards) only as long as needed.
- Build first mesh node / dish using personal or LLC funds → log as first verified work.
- Publish research, processes, and builds to GitHub + Zenodo for immutable provenance.
- Apply for grants and partnerships using published artifacts.
- Ask landlords for rent history letters to boost credit while still in old system.

**Phase 2: Begin Dual-System Operation (Months 7-18)**
- First ACRE mints from verified physical work (construction, node deployment, care work).
- Begin receiving lifetime affiliate rewards from projects you help build.
- Reduce reliance on credit cards as ACRE + affiliate income grows.
- Participate in first 501c3 preparedness projects (land work, freeze-drying setup, shelter construction).

**Phase 3: Primary OpenRoot Economy (Years 2-3)**
- Majority of income and reputation comes from Contribution Ledger.
- Housing transitions to self-built or crew-built geodesic domes.
- Banking largely replaced by Solana wallets + mesh relay.
- Credit score becomes secondary or irrelevant in OpenRoot zones.

**Phase 4: Full Post-Extraction Operation (Year 4+)**
- Old-system instruments used only when absolutely necessary for external interfacing.
- Primary identity, reputation, income, and housing all native to OpenRoot systems.
- Lifetime affiliate income + team mutual aid replace pensions, welfare, and most government support.

## Part 2: 501c3 Preparedness Entity Foundation

**Entity Name (suggested):** OpenRoot Preparedness & Resilience Foundation (or similar)

**Core Mission**
Acquire land, preserve food/medicines/protein for long-term resilience, and construct near-indestructible community shelters using advanced ferrocement stress-skin technology. Operate as a nonprofit that supports the broader OpenRoot mission while providing tangible preparedness infrastructure.

**Key Activities**
- Land acquisition for food forests, seed banks, and shelter clusters.
- Freeze-drying and long-term storage of organically grown local produce, herbs, and proteins (25+ year shelf life).
- Construction of monolithic catenary curve / double-curve ferrocement stress-skin domes filled with aerated cellular concrete for insulation.
- These shelters are designed as near-indestructible community refuges for hardship, drought, economic collapse, or other systemic shocks.
- Integration with H-003 thermal cascades and fractal mesh for self-sufficient operation.

**Governance & Integration**
- 501c3 board initially drawn from early OpenRoot contributors and teams of 50.
- All major projects logged on the Contribution Ledger.
- Builders and caregivers on 501c3 projects receive ACRE mints + lifetime affiliate rewards.
- Land and infrastructure held by the 501c3 for community benefit rather than private ownership.
- Transparent public reporting of assets, projects, and impact.

**Relationship to Broader OpenRoot**
The 501c3 acts as the physical preparedness and land-holding arm, while the DAO / Contribution Ledger handles coordination, reputation, and token economics. They reinforce each other: the 501c3 provides real-world resilience infrastructure; the ledger provides the economic and governance layer that makes large-scale cooperation possible without traditional government or banking.

## Part 3: Advanced Shelter Technology — Monolithic Catenary / Double-Curve Ferrocement Stress-Skin

**Design Overview**
- Primary structure: Monolithic ferrocement stress-skin using catenary and double-curve geometry for maximum strength with minimal material.
- Insulation: Filled with aerated cellular concrete (lightweight, high thermal performance).
- Result: Near-indestructible, low-maintenance, highly insulated shelters suitable as community refuges in hard times.
- Construction method: Compatible with 50-person crews and AE-GFRC pumping techniques already documented.

**Integration Benefits**
- Excellent passive solar performance when combined with upward/outward bevel architecture.
- Natural compatibility with H-003 thermal cascades (thermal mass + underground connections).
- Can serve as hardened nodes in the fractal mesh.
- Long lifespan reduces long-term resource demand.
