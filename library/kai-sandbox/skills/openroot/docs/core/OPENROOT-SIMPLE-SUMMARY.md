# OpenRoot — Simple Explanation for Everyone

**The Big Idea in One Sentence**  
OpenRoot is a plan to let regular people own and run their own internet and communication network — so money that currently leaves your town every month to big corporations can stay in your community instead.

**The Problem We’re Solving**  
Right now, most families pay $70–$150 every month for internet and phone service. That money goes to a handful of giant companies. It leaves your town, your state, and your country. In return, you get service that can be slow, expensive, or shut off if the company or government decides to. Rural areas often get left behind because companies don’t make enough profit there.

OpenRoot asks a simple question: What if communities built and owned their own communication system instead?

**What OpenRoot Actually Is**  
It’s a do-it-yourself communication network that anyone can help build. It uses simple, cheap equipment (like a special kind of antenna made from plywood and chicken wire that costs about $40) plus open-source software. These small stations connect to each other like a web, so if one goes down, others keep working.

The network is designed to grow naturally. One person or family puts up a station. Their neighbors see it works and add their own. Over time it spreads across towns, states, and eventually the whole country — and even connects with similar networks around the world.

**How It Helps Regular People**  
- You pay once for equipment instead of every month forever.
- Money stays local instead of going to distant corporations.
- The network is harder to shut down or control from the top.
- Rural areas and small towns get the same quality connection as big cities.
- Communities can use the saved money for schools, clinics, farms, or small businesses.

**How We Actually Build It**  
It starts small. In Sikeston, Missouri, there’s a plan to put the first station on an existing kiosk at the highway interchange. Once that one works, nearby households can add their own simple stations. Groups of about 50 people (including builders, cooks, medical support, and coordinators who look out for each other) travel to help set up stations in new areas. Everyone on these teams gets paid fairly and the group takes care of itself.

The whole worldwide system can be built in stages over a few years, mostly by regular people using basic tools.

**Why This Matters**  
Right now, billions of dollars leave communities every year for communication services. OpenRoot is a way to keep that money circulating locally while giving people more control and better resilience. It’s not about waiting for big companies or the government to fix things. It’s about communities choosing to build something better for themselves.

**The Heart of It**  
OpenRoot is based on two simple principles:
1. Take care of each other (called “agape” — unconditional mutual care).
2. Build things that last and that anyone can understand and improve.

It’s not a company. It’s not an app. It’s a shared project that starts with one station in one town and grows from there — owned by the people who use it.

One Human Family.  
Start where you are. Build what you can. Take care of each other.
