# Abstract — Thermal Cascade Radiative Cooling System

We present a novel thermal energy storage architecture utilizing multi-night radiative cooling accumulation against the deep space heat sink (≈3 K). A 10 m² emissive panel operating under clear sky conditions captures approximately 13 kWh of thermal exergy per night via selective long-wave infrared emission. This cold accumulates across seven consecutive nights within insulated concrete thermal batteries (total volume 60 m³, U-value 0.05 W·m⁻²·K⁻¹), yielding stored exergy between 70–91 kWh after accounting for conductive losses. Discharge through cascaded conversion stages (thermoelectric generators at 15%, Stirling engines at 30%, Rankine cycles at 35%) produces sustained electrical output of 21–27 kWh over 8-hour windows at peak power delivery of 3–4 kW. By exploiting the cosmic microwave background as a heat sink rather than ambient air, this approach achieves a theoretical Carnot ceiling of 98.9% compared to conventional atmospheric-sink systems (17.1%), representing a 5.8× improvement in thermodynamic potential. Simulation code and verification data are publicly archived under identifier UNE:TH.CAL.TCR.V02.

Keywords — radiative cooling, deep-space heat sink, thermal battery, Stirling cycle, exergy accumulation, off-grid energy.
