# Cement Basics — What It Is and Why It Sets
**UNE:** DV.CON.FUND.CEMENT.001  
**Status:** fundamental  
**η note:** Everything above this file rests on these facts. No mysticism, only chemistry and physics.

## One-sentence essence
Portland cement is a manufactured calcium-silicate powder that reacts with water to form a stone-like solid through hydration; the reaction is irreversible under normal conditions and produces the binder in all our GFRC and aerated mixes.

## What Cement Actually Is
- Made by heating limestone (CaCO₃) + clay/silica sources to \~1450 °C in a kiln.
- The resulting clinker is ground with a small amount of gypsum.
- Main compounds (Bogue):
  - C₃S (alite) — rapid strength
  - C₂S (belite) — long-term strength
  - C₃A — early set, heat
  - C₄AF — flux, color

## Why It Sets (Hydration)
When water is added:
1. C₃S and C₂S react with water to form calcium-silicate-hydrate (C-S-H) gel + calcium hydroxide.
2. C-S-H is the actual glue — a nano-scale, disordered binder that fills space and locks particles together.
3. The reaction is exothermic (gives off heat).
4. Once formed, C-S-H does not dissolve back under normal pH and temperature. The set is permanent.

## Key Practical Facts for Our Work
- Water/cement ratio controls both strength and porosity. Lower w/c → higher strength, lower permeability.
- Too much water = capillary pores = weak, permeable concrete.
- Gypsum is added to prevent flash set from C₃A.
- In GFRC and aerated mixes we deliberately keep w/c low (0.32–0.38) and use polymer + high-shear to maintain workability.

## Why This Matters for AeroCement / Open-Cell Work
The solid matrix whose strength appears in the Gibson–Ashby and power-law formulas is this C-S-H + unhydrated cement + sand.  
Everything we do with bubbles, fibers, and black locust is an attempt to organize that matrix more efficiently around air.
