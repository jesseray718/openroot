# AX-018, AX-019 & PO-014, PO-015 — Agape Formalized

Source: agape.md (Fractal Convergence session dump). Status of this doc: concept, with tested code findings inline.

## Axioms

AX-018: Benefit is measured at the recipient, never the actor. A unit of good exists only where someone other than the claimant received it. (This is the operational definition of agape: self-giving for the good of the other, made countable.)

AX-019: A benefit claim enters the ledger as verified only when attested by at least two nodes other than the claimant. Unverified benefit contributes zero to good/input.

## Postulates

PO-014: In a payoff structure where reward is an increasing function of verified benefit-to-other, cooperation strictly dominates defection for any node whose planning horizon exceeds one epoch. [concept] — NOTE: this dominance holds BY CONSTRUCTION; we defined the payoffs. It is a design theorem, not a discovery about human nature. The open empirical question is whether real participants accept and remain in such a payoff structure. Prior art says the mechanism is sound in principle: indirect reciprocity sustains cooperation when reputation is tracked (Nowak, "Five Rules for the Evolution of Cooperation," Science 2006; Axelrod, The Evolution of Cooperation, 1984).

PO-015: "Entropy → 0, comfort → ∞" is the limit behavior of the model's index, not a thermodynamic claim. [concept] Physical entropy still increases; the slump term approaches zero only through continuous maintenance labor, which is itself an input the denominator must count. Earlier phrasing ("heat death of civilization is reversible") is retracted as physics and retained only as a statement about the maintained-infrastructure index.

## What survives review

The core mechanism is genuinely strong and has serious prior art: reward keyed to other-verified benefit is the same family as indirect-reciprocity reputation systems, and the four ACRE governance layers map closely onto Ostrom's design principles for commons governance (Governing the Commons, 1990): auditability = monitoring; two-validator rule = graduated, peer-based verification; quadratic voting (Lalley & Weyl; Posner & Weyl, Radical Markets, 2018) = collective-choice arrangements; voluntary participation = clearly defined membership. Sorokin's Harvard Research Center in Creative Altruism (The Ways and Power of Love, 1954) is the closest historical attempt to operationalize agape as a system variable — cite him. Theological grounding: agape in Nygren (Agape and Eros, 1930–36) and Lewis (The Four Loves, 1960).
