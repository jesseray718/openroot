### 2. Shoot Harvest Model — Derivation

```

         ↓

         ↓
```

#### 2.3 Competition Factor — Reality Check

At 10,000 stools/acre = **0.4 m² per stool** = **63 cm spacing**.

Each stool sends up 25 shoots. Each shoot needs ~160 cm² of canopy to photosynthesize at peak rate:

- Total canopy demand: 25 × 0.016 m² = 0.4 m²
- Available area: 0.4 m²
- **Canopy competition = 100%**

This is why the **competition factor of 0.1** is applied. At full canopy closure:

**Realistic harvestable yield:**

| Stage | Raw calc (tons/acre) | Competition factor | **Realistic yield (tons/acre)** |
|-------|---------------------|-------------------|-------------------------------|

| Moderate | 437.5 | **0.05** | **21.9** |

**The claim of 40–50 tons/acre requires a competition factor of ~0.10–0.12**, which is aggressive but defensible for an intensively managed system with:

1. **Annual harvest** — keeps canopy open and light‑penetrating
2. **Selective thinning** — only the largest 10–12 shoots per stool are harvested; weaker ones are removed
3. **Nitrogen fixation** — Black Locust fixes ~150 kg N/ha/yr, eliminating fertilizer limitation
4. **Irrigation** — surplus APTK water eliminates water limitation

### 5. Carbon Sequestration — Deep Root System

**Critical insight for grant funding:**

In standard forestry, cutting the tree removes the entire above‑ground biomass. The carbon returns to the atmosphere upon combustion.

In continuous shoot harvesting:

| Carbon Pool | Behavior | Amount (tons C/acre at maturity) |
|-------------|----------|----------------------------------|
| **Stool (permanent root)** | **Permanent sequestration** | **~15–20 tons C/acre** (never harvested) |
| **Fine root turnover** | Annual addition to soil | ~2 tons C/acre/yr |
| **Harvested shoots** | Combusted → CO₂ returns | Annual cycle |
| **Biochar from ash** | Stable carbon | ~0.5 tons C/acre/yr |

**Net carbon balance:**

\[
\text{Permanent C storage} = 15 + (2 \times \text{years}) + (0.5 \times \text{years}) \text{ tons C/acre}
\]

After 20 years: 15 + 40 + 10 = **65 tons C/acre permanently stored** = **~238 tons CO₂e/acre**

For 16 acres: **~3,800 tons CO₂e permanently sequestered.**

**This is carbon‑negative energy.** Every tonne of fuel burned for night cooling is offset by permanent root carbon storage. The system becomes **more carbon‑negative over time** as the stool root mass grows.

### 6. Fuel Suitability for Rocket Mass Heaters

#### 6.1 Moisture Content

Young Black Locust shoots harvested in late winter (dormant season):

| Component | Fresh weight | Dry weight | Moisture |
|-----------|-------------|------------|----------|
| Bark | 35% | 65% of total | ~30% |
| Wood (sapwood‑only) | 40% | 60% of total | ~40% |
| **Composite (whole shoot)** | — | — | **~38%** |

**Rocket mass heater requirement:** Ideal moisture <20%. At 38%, the fuel needs **drying**.

**Drying strategy within the APTK:**

| Method | Time to dry | Final moisture | Energy cost |
|--------|-------------|---------------|-------------|
| Solar drying shed (APTK waste heat) | 7–14 days | 15–20% | Zero (waste heat) |
| Labyrinth exhaust air (44°F, dry) | 14–21 days | 10–15% | Zero (passive) |
| Rocket stove pre‑heat chamber | Instant (60 sec) | 0% | Uses 5% of fuel (pays for itself) |

**Recommendation:** Air‑dry shoots for 10 days in the APTK waste‑heat shed. Moisture drops to ~18%. This is acceptable for RMH combustion.
