# PO-026 — The Cashback Subsidy

**Domain:** Credit & Business (CB)
**Type:** Postulate
**Status:** Active

## Statement

At sufficient spending volume through cashback credit cards (2% or greater), the cashback return exceeds the cost of credit-building subscriptions (e.g., $35/mo Kikoff). At this threshold, the credit-building infrastructure becomes net-positive — it generates more money than it costs. The system transitions from expense to revenue source.

## Derivation

Break-even: $35/mo Kikoff ÷ 0.02 (2% cashback) = $1,750/mo spending required. At $1,750/month routed through a 2% cashback card, cashback = $35/month = Kikoff subscription cost. Above $1,750/month, the system generates net positive cash flow. At $2,500/mo spending, cashback = $50 — system generates $15/month profit.

## Application

When monthly spending through cashback cards exceeds $1,750, the credit-building system becomes self-funding. Every dollar spent above break-even increases net yield. The infrastructure that builds the credit also pays for itself.
