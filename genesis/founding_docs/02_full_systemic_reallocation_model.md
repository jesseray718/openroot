This document consolidates the major extraction streams currently leaving communities and shows what becomes possible when that wealth is redirected into local, life-affirming systems (communication mesh, thermal energy, health protocols, seed banks, community workshops, and tool libraries).

## Major Extraction Streams (Annual Global Estimates)

| Extraction Stream                    | Annual Global Extraction | Notes |
|--------------------------------------|---------------------------|-------|
| Telecommunications                   | \~$1.7 trillion            | Corporate ISPs and mobile carriers |
| Centralized Energy (electricity + heating/cooling) | \~$2.5–3 trillion     | Utilities and fuel companies |
| Transportation Fuels                 | \~$3 trillion+             | Gasoline, diesel, jet fuel |
| Pharmaceuticals                      | \~$1.5–1.8 trillion        | Patented drugs + related medical extraction |
| Policing + Prison Industrial Complex | \~$1–1.5 trillion          | Global spending on prisons, policing, and related systems (US alone \~$300B+) |
| Military-Industrial Complex          | \~$2.4 trillion            | Global military spending (US \~$800B–1T+) |
| **Total Annual Extraction**          | **\~$12–13.5 trillion**    | Roughly 12–15%+ of global GDP |

These numbers represent wealth that is currently extracted from communities and concentrated upward. When communities implement local systems (fractal mesh, H-003 thermal cascades, community health protocols, local food/herb production, and mutual-aid organization), most of this extraction can be stopped or dramatically reduced.

## What Gets Reallocated When Extraction Ends

When communities build and maintain their own systems, the money that used to leave can instead support:

- Local communication infrastructure (mesh nodes)
- Local thermal + solar energy systems
- Local health and detox protocols (spirulina, mucilaginous fibers, herbalism, Rick Simpson oil, etc.)
- Seed banks and medicinal plant breeding programs
- Community workshops and tool libraries
- Long-term food preservation systems (dehydrators, freeze-drying)
- Teams of 50 organized around mutual care and productive work
- Transparent, victim-focused security instead of mass incarceration

Even if only 30–50% of these streams are successfully localized, the annual reallocation would still be in the multiple trillions — enough to transform communities worldwide within a generation.

## What This Means Practically

**For a single community (example: Sikeston-scale area):**
- Current combined extraction (telecom + energy + fuel + pharma + policing share) is in the tens of millions per year.
- Redirecting even a portion of that into local mesh, thermal systems, health protocols, seed banks, and workshops creates self-reinforcing prosperity.
- Households keep thousands of dollars per year that used to leave.
- The community gains the ability to fund its own infrastructure, health, education, and productive capacity without relying on distant governments or corporations.

**At national and global scale:**
- Trillions of dollars currently used to maintain extraction systems (corporate telecom, fossil fuel infrastructure, pharmaceutical monopolies, mass incarceration, and permanent war economies) could instead maintain and expand life-giving systems.
- Seed banks, community workshops, tool libraries, and long-term food/medicinal herb preservation become well-funded public goods instead of afterthoughts.
- The need for many current policing and prison functions decreases as communities become healthier, more connected, and better resourced through teams of 50 organized around mutual aid.

## Core Principle

We are not proposing to eliminate necessary functions. We are proposing to stop the extraction of wealth through systems that often create or prolong the very problems they claim to solve. When communities control their own communication, energy, health knowledge, food systems, and mutual support structures, the economic justification for many extractive institutions largely disappears.

The numbers show that the resources already exist. The question is whether we will continue feeding extraction machines or redirect those same resources into building resilient, caring, and productive communities.
