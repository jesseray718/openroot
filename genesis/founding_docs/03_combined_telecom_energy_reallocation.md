## The H-003 Thermosolar Thermal Blackbody System

Open-cell aerocement acts as a volumetric blackbody absorber. Sunlight is captured as heat. Air flows through the porous material and is heated. Hot air rises naturally into an underground labyrinth of wet aerocement where the stable cool earth temperature creates both cooling and heat storage. The temperature difference (Delta T) between hot and cool sections can drive a Stirling engine for mechanical work and electricity generation with almost no moving parts and no fuel.

This system produces heating, cooling, mechanical power, and electricity from sunlight and the day/night + surface/underground temperature difference.

## Delta T Vehicle (Temperature-Difference Powered Vehicle)

A Delta T vehicle uses a Stirling engine powered by the temperature difference created by the H-003 system (or a vehicle-mounted version of the same principle). It does not burn gasoline or diesel. It runs on heat differential.

**Is it invented?**  
Stirling engines have existed since 1816. They have been used in experimental vehicles, submarines, and solar power systems for decades. What is new in OpenRoot is integrating a high-efficiency, low-maintenance Delta T drive directly with community-scale thermosolar thermal storage. The core technology exists. The integrated, community-owned version is being designed and documented now.

**Does this make gasoline and oil obsolete?**  
Not 100% immediately, but their dominance ends. 
- Personal cars, light trucks, and many delivery vehicles can switch to Delta T or electric (charged by the same thermal + solar system).
- Long-haul trucking, aviation, and shipping will take longer and may retain some liquid fuels for decades.
- Overall, the vast majority of daily transportation energy can shift to local, fuel-free thermal and solar sources within 10–20 years in communities that build the system.

Gasoline and oil do not disappear overnight, but the economic and strategic power of oil monopolies collapses when most daily movement no longer requires their product.
